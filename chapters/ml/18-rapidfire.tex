\chapter{Rapid-fire}
\label{ch:rapid}
Ninety-seven questions in the form they are actually asked across the table, with answers you could say in under
twenty seconds. Each points back to the chapter that justifies it. Cover the answers and test yourself; if an
answer surprises you, read the chapter, not the answer.

\newcounter{rf}
\newcommand{\rf}[1]{\par\smallskip\noindent\refstepcounter{rf}\textbf{\therf.\ #1}\enskip}

{\small
	\section{Setup, maths and linear models}
	\prototype{``Is $R^2=0.64$ good?'' --- ``It means 64\% of the variance of $y$ is explained by a linear fit; whether
		that is good depends on the noise in the problem.''}

	\rf{Loss versus risk?} Loss scores one prediction; risk is its expectation over the data distribution;
	empirical risk is the average over your sample, which ERM minimises.

	\rf{What is the generalisation gap?} Test (or validation) error minus training error. A large gap means
	overfitting; a small gap with high error means underfitting.

	\rf{Why three splits instead of two?} Choosing among models on the validation set biases its score upwards;
	the test set must play no part in any choice so its score stays unbiased.

	\rf{What is Bayes error?} The error of the best possible classifier for the distribution --- the irreducible
	floor set by overlap of the classes, not the error of naive Bayes.

	\rf{Why is the 0-1 loss not used for training?} It is non-convex and piecewise constant, so its gradient is
	zero almost everywhere; we minimise convex surrogates (log, hinge, exponential) that upper-bound it.

	\rf{Is $\max$ of convex functions convex? $\min$?} Max yes (epigraph is an intersection); min generally no ---
	it can have two separate valleys.

	\rf{Is $\sin x$ convex? $\abs x$?} $\sin x$ no (the chord from $0$ to $\pi$ lies below it); $\abs x$ yes, though not
	differentiable at $0$.

	\rf{$r=-0.8$; what is $R^2$ of a simple linear fit?} $0.64$; the sign disappears.

	\rf{Covariance versus correlation?} Covariance has units and depends on scale; correlation is covariance divided
	by both standard deviations, unit-free, in $[-1,1]$, linear association only.

	\rf{Zero correlation means independence?} No: $y=x^2$ with symmetric $x$ has $r=0$ and total dependence.

	\rf{Least squares from MLE?} Gaussian noise makes the negative log-likelihood proportional to the sum of squared
	residuals.

	\rf{Ridge from a prior?} MAP with a zero-mean Gaussian prior on the weights; $\lambda=\sigma^2/\tau^2$. Laplace prior
	gives lasso.

	\rf{Normal equations?} $\X\T\X\w=\X\T\y$; the residual is orthogonal to the column space of $\X$.

	\rf{Why not invert $\X\T\X$?} Forming it squares the condition number; solve with QR or SVD.

	\rf{When is $\X\T\X$ singular?} Perfect collinearity or more features than rows; ridge fixes it.

	\rf{What does multicollinearity damage?} The variance and interpretability of individual coefficients, not the
	predictions in the data range. Diagnose with VIF.

	\rf{Can $R^2$ be negative?} On test data, yes: worse than predicting the training mean. On training data with an
	intercept, no.

	\rf{Why adjusted $R^2$?} Plain $R^2$ never falls when you add features, even noise; adjusted $R^2$ penalises the
	count.

	\rf{Gauss--Markov in one line?} With zero-mean, uncorrelated, equal-variance errors, OLS is the minimum-variance
	linear unbiased estimator --- biased estimators like ridge can still have lower MSE.

	\section{Regularisation and classifiers}
	\prototype{``Increase $\lambda$: what happens to training error?'' --- ``It cannot go down.''}

	\rf{Bias--variance decomposition?} Expected squared test error $=$ noise $+$ bias$^2+$ variance.

	\rf{More data reduces bias or variance?} Variance. It cannot fix a model class that cannot represent the truth.

	\rf{Effect of $\lambda$ in $E+\lambda\sum w^2$?} Larger $\lambda$: smaller weights, higher bias, lower variance,
	training error up. $\lambda\to0$: unregularised fit; $\lambda\to\infty$: constant prediction.

	\rf{Why does lasso give zeros and ridge not?} The $\ell_1$ subgradient at $0$ is an interval, so small
	coefficients are thresholded to exactly zero; the $\ell_2$ penalty's derivative vanishes at $0$ and only shrinks.

	\rf{When elastic net?} Correlated groups of features: lasso picks one arbitrarily, elastic net shares weight and
	stays sparse.

	\rf{Must you scale before ridge or lasso?} Yes: the penalty treats all coefficients alike, so units matter.

	\rf{What is double descent?} Test error peaks where the model can just interpolate ($p\approx n$) and falls again as
	parameters grow, for minimum-norm solutions.

	\rf{Is logistic regression a linear classifier?} Yes; its boundary $\w\T\x+b=0$ is a hyperplane.

	\rf{Gradient of log-loss?} $\X\T(\bm p-\y)/n$ --- prediction minus target, times input.

	\rf{Why not MSE for logistic regression?} Non-convex in the weights and its gradient vanishes when the model is
	confidently wrong; log-loss is the Bernoulli likelihood and convex.

	\rf{What does a coefficient of $0.7$ mean in logistic regression?} One unit of that feature multiplies the odds by
	$e^{0.7}\approx2$.

	\rf{What happens with perfectly separable data?} The unregularised MLE does not exist; weights grow forever.
	Regularise.

	\rf{OvR versus OvO for 10 classes?} 10 versus 45 binary classifiers; softmax regression needs neither.

	\rf{Can a perceptron learn XOR?} No; XOR is not linearly separable. Add a feature $x_1x_2$ or a hidden layer.

	\rf{Naive Bayes' assumption?} Features are conditionally independent given the class.

	\rf{Why Laplace smoothing?} An unseen word would make the whole product zero; adding pseudo-counts is a MAP
	estimate under a Dirichlet prior.

	\rf{Why are naive Bayes probabilities bad but its accuracy fine?} Correlated features are double-counted, pushing
	probabilities to the extremes, but the argmax is often still right.

	\rf{LDA versus QDA?} Shared covariance gives a linear boundary; class-specific covariances give a quadratic one with
	many more parameters.

	\rf{$k=1$ in $k$-NN?} Zero training error, highest variance. Large $k$: smoother, more bias.

	\rf{What is the curse of dimensionality?} In high dimensions distances concentrate and neighbourhoods stop being
	local, so distance-based methods need exponentially more data.

	\rf{Why do KD-trees fail in high dimensions?} The query ball crosses most splitting planes, so pruning stops working.

	\section{SVMs, trees and ensembles}
	\prototype{``Bagging versus boosting?'' --- ``Parallel averaging to cut variance versus sequential correction to cut
		bias.''}

	\rf{What are support vectors?} Training points with non-zero dual weight: on the margin, inside it, or misclassified.
	Only they define the boundary.

	\rf{What does $C$ do in an SVM?} Penalty on margin violations; large $C$ means narrow margin and low bias, small $C$
	wide margin and more regularisation.

	\rf{What does $\gamma$ do in an RBF kernel?} Inverse length scale: large $\gamma$ makes boundaries wiggle around
	individual points.

	\rf{Kernel trick?} The dual needs only inner products; a kernel computes $\phi(\x)\T\phi(\z)$ without forming
	$\phi$.

	\rf{What makes a function a valid kernel?} Every Gram matrix it produces is symmetric positive semi-definite.

	\rf{Why the dual?} Kernels, sparsity in $\alpha$, and one variable per example.

	\rf{Is the hinge loss convex?} Yes, though not differentiable at margin 1.

	\rf{Entropy of a 50/50 node? Gini?} 1 bit; 0.5.

	\rf{Why gain ratio?} Information gain favours many-valued features (an ID column gives perfect gain).

	\rf{Do trees need feature scaling?} No; splits are invariant to monotone transforms.

	\rf{Can a regression tree extrapolate?} No; leaves predict averages of training targets.

	\rf{How do you prune?} Pre-prune with depth and leaf-size limits, or cost-complexity prune with $\alpha$ chosen by CV.

	\rf{Probability a row is in a bootstrap sample?} $1-(1-1/n)^n\to1-1/e\approx63.2\%$.

	\rf{Does a random forest reduce bias?} No --- variance, by averaging decorrelated deep trees.

	\rf{Can more trees overfit a random forest?} No; error stabilises. More boosting rounds can overfit.

	\rf{What decorrelates a forest's trees?} Bootstrap rows plus a random subset of features at each split.

	\rf{What is OOB error?} Error on each row using only trees that did not see it; a free validation estimate.

	\rf{What is wrong with MDI importance?} It is biased towards high-cardinality and noisy continuous features and
	splits credit among correlated ones; use permutation importance on held-out data.

	\rf{AdaBoost's learner weight?} $\alpha=\frac12\ln\frac{1-\eps}{\eps}$; misclassified points are up-weighted by
	$e^{\alpha}$.

	\rf{Gradient boosting in one sentence?} Fit each new tree to the negative gradient of the loss at the current
	predictions and add a shrunken copy.

	\rf{XGBoost leaf weight and gain?} $w^\star=-G/(H+\lambda)$; gain $=\frac12[\frac{G_L^2}{H_L+\lambda}+
	\frac{G_R^2}{H_R+\lambda}-\frac{G^2}{H+\lambda}]-\gamma$.

	\rf{What do $\lambda$ and $\gamma$ do in XGBoost?} $\lambda$ shrinks leaf values; $\gamma$ is the minimum gain needed
	for a split.

	\rf{How does XGBoost handle missing values?} Each split learns a default direction for them.

	\rf{LightGBM versus XGBoost?} Histogram splits and leaf-wise growth by default, plus gradient-based sampling and
	feature bundling; usually faster, can overfit small data.

	\rf{Learning rate versus number of trees?} Smaller rate needs more trees and generalises better; use early stopping.

	\section{Unsupervised, evaluation and data}
	\prototype{``Accuracy is 97\%. Good?'' --- ``What is the positive rate? If it is 3\%, a constant predictor scores the
		same.''}

	\rf{When does $k$-means stop?} When assignments stop changing (or centroids move less than a tolerance, or
	iterations run out). It always stops, at a local minimum.

	\rf{Why $k$-means++?} Spreads initial centroids, avoiding bad local minima, with a provable approximation bound.

	\rf{$k$-means assumptions?} Roughly spherical, similar-sized clusters in a Euclidean space; scale features first.

	\rf{DBSCAN over $k$-means when?} Arbitrary shapes, unknown $k$, outliers to flag.

	\rf{EM in one line?} E-step: compute responsibilities; M-step: weighted MLE; likelihood never decreases.

	\rf{Why standardise before PCA?} PCA maximises variance, so the feature with the largest units dominates otherwise.

	\rf{PCA versus ICA?} PCA finds uncorrelated directions of maximal variance; ICA finds statistically independent,
	non-Gaussian sources.

	\rf{Can you read cluster distances off a t-SNE plot?} No; it preserves local neighbourhoods, not global geometry.

	\rf{Lift?} Confidence divided by the consequent's support; above 1 means positive association.

	\rf{Precision versus recall?} Of flagged, how many are right; of actual positives, how many are flagged.

	\rf{F1?} Harmonic mean of precision and recall; $F_\beta$ weights recall $\beta$ times as much.

	\rf{Macro versus micro F1?} Macro averages per-class F1 equally; micro pools counts and equals accuracy for
	single-label multiclass.

	\rf{What does AUC measure?} The probability that a random positive is scored above a random negative.

	\rf{ROC or PR curve for rare positives?} PR; ROC looks excellent because false positives are few relative to a huge
	negative class.

	\rf{Is a high-AUC model calibrated?} Not necessarily; AUC ignores calibration. Use reliability plots, Platt or
	isotonic scaling.

	\rf{Cross-validating a time series?} Forward-chaining splits; never shuffle.

	\rf{Give three kinds of leakage.} Preprocessing on all data before splitting; features from after the outcome;
	the same entity in train and test.

	\rf{Why nested CV?} The best inner CV score is optimistic; the outer loop estimates the whole tuning procedure.

	\rf{AIC versus BIC?} Both are $-2\ln\hat L$ plus a penalty; BIC's $k\ln n$ is harsher and picks smaller models.

	\rf{What is a p-value?} The probability, if the null hypothesis is true, of data at least as extreme as observed.
	Not the probability the null is true.

	\rf{Type I versus Type II?} False positive versus false negative; power is $1-$Type II rate.

	\rf{Paired or two-sample $t$?} Paired when the same units are measured twice; it removes between-unit variation.

	\rf{Twenty tests at 5\%?} About a 64\% chance of at least one false positive; use Bonferroni or Benjamini--Hochberg.

	\rf{Simpson's paradox?} A trend in every subgroup reverses in the aggregate because of a confounder.

	\rf{Which models need scaling?} $k$-NN, SVM, $k$-means, PCA, regularised and gradient-trained linear models and
	neural nets; not trees.

	\rf{Target encoding risk?} Leakage: encode out-of-fold and smooth, or validation scores lie.

	\rf{Missing values?} Ask why they are missing; impute inside folds, add an indicator when missingness may be
	informative, or use models that handle them natively.

	\rf{When should SMOTE be applied?} Only inside training folds, never before splitting or to test data.

	\rf{What does IDF do?} Down-weights terms that occur in many documents: $\log(N/\text{df})$.

	\rf{Why does momentum help?} It averages out oscillating gradient components, improving the dependence on the
	condition number from $\kappa$ to $\sqrt\kappa$.

	\rf{Why decay the SGD step?} With a constant step SGD hovers in a noise ball around the optimum.

	\rf{Newton versus gradient descent?} Newton uses curvature and converges quadratically near the optimum but costs
	$O(d^3)$ per step; L-BFGS approximates it cheaply.
